​​**SMART FESTIVALS AS EXPERIENCE ENGINES: LINKING TECHNOLOGY EVALUATIONS,
ANTICIPATED PERSONALISATION, IMMERSION AND LOYALTY INTENTIONS**

**Abstract**

Smart festivals increasingly rely on digital infrastructures (apps,
access control, cashless systems and immersive activations) to reduce
friction and deliver personalised, emotionally engaging experiences.
Because many festivalgoers have limited exposure to advanced festival
technologies, this study examines adoption readiness through anticipated
evaluations of smart festival technologies and their links to perceived
personalisation, anticipated immersion and loyalty intentions. Using a
survey of festival attendees (N = 338) and covariance-based SEM, results
show that perceived ease of use strongly shapes perceived usefulness,
while usefulness and trust are key antecedents of perceived
personalisation. Personalisation, in turn, intensifies anticipated
immersion and strengthens loyalty both directly and indirectly via
attitude and intention to use. Privacy concerns display a small but
significant positive association with perceived personalisation,
consistent with a privacy--personalisation tension in data-intensive
festival settings. Findings translate into actionable design and
governance principles for delivering ethical personalisation that
enhances immersion and repeat attendance.

smart festivals; personalisation; immersive experience; technology
acceptance; trust; privacy concerns; adoption readiness; loyalty
intentions.

## Statement of Contribution to Events-Related Research {#statement-of-contribution-to-events-related-research .unnumbered}

This paper contributes to the Special Issue on "Personalisation and
Immersive Experiences in Events" by explaining how smart festival
technologies translate into personalised and immersive outcomes that
matter for loyalty. Festivals constitute high-density, time-pressured
environments where usability frictions can quickly undermine experience
quality, yet research often treats technology adoption as a generic
service context. We address this gap by positioning "smart festivals" as
distinctive experience settings and by integrating technology acceptance
beliefs with two experiential mechanisms: perceived personalisation and
technology-enabled immersion.

Conceptually, the study extends TAM by modelling personalisation as a
value mechanism linking perceived usefulness and trust to immersion and,
ultimately, loyalty intentions. This approach connects acceptance-based
pathways (attitude and intention to use) with experience-based pathways
(immersion), clarifying how "smartness" becomes experiential value and
behavioural loyalty in festival contexts. The model also incorporates
privacy concerns to reflect the governance and ethical tensions inherent
to data-intensive festival infrastructures.

Empirically, we test the model using survey data from festival attendees
(N = 338) and covariance-based SEM. Results show that usefulness and
trust are key antecedents of perceived personalisation, which strongly
increases anticipated immersion and influences loyalty largely through
indirect pathways via attitude and intention. Privacy concerns display a
small positive association with personalisation, suggesting a
privacy--personalisation tension rather than a purely inhibiting effect.
Practically, the findings translate into design and governance
principles for organisers seeking to deliver ethical personalisation,
deepen immersion, and strengthen revisit and recommendation intentions
under peak festival operating conditions.

1.  **Introduction**

Festivals are increasingly managed as high-density, time-pressured
environments in which attendees continuously make on-site decisions
(where to go next, what to prioritise, how to avoid queues and
crowding). At the same time, the events industry faces growing
expectations for meaningful, emotionally engaging and highly
personalised experiences, while organisers operate under constraints and
uncertainty that intensify the need for innovation and resilience (Azara
et al., 2023; Michopoulou et al., 2023). In response, "smart festival"
infrastructures: festival apps, QR/RFID access control, cashless
payments, real-time updates, and interactive or immersive activations
are increasingly deployed to streamline operations and enhance the
attendee journey (Cimbaljević et al., 2019; Neuhofer et al., 2020). In
these contexts, technology is not merely an add-on: it can shape the
lived festival experience by reducing friction and increasing relevance.

Smart festival technologies can function as operant resources that
enable value co-creation among organisers, attendees and stakeholders,
but they can also trigger co-destruction when usability fails under peak
demand or when data practices are perceived as opaque (Azara et al.,
2018; Wallace & Michopoulou, 2023). This duality is particularly salient
because many smart functionalities are data-intensive. Privacy concerns
reflect apprehensions about data collection and loss of control
(Malhotra et al., 2004) and often operate as perceived "costs" in a
privacy calculus where users weigh expected benefits against perceived
risks (Awad & Krishnan, 2006). In event contexts, such tensions are
evident in adoption evaluations of identification and recognition
systems, where trust and privacy considerations shape willingness to
engage with technology (Norfolk & O'Regan, 2020; Ciftci et al., 2024).
Accordingly, the smart festival challenge is not only adoption, but
experience governance: how to deliver personalisation and immersion
without undermining trust and perceived legitimacy.

While festival research has long linked immersion, memorability and
meaning to revisit and recommendation intentions (Van Winkle & Falk,
2015; Iversen et al., 2023), and event technology studies show that
perceived usefulness and ease of use remain relevant predictors of
attitudes and intentions (Talantis et al., 2020; Norfolk & O'Regan,
2020), limited work integrates these streams to explain how acceptance
beliefs translate into the personalised and immersive outcomes**.**
Moreover, advanced festival technologies evolve rapidly, and attendees
often have uneven exposure to AI-enabled guidance or immersive
activations. This makes it important to examine adoption readiness
through anticipated evaluations formed prior to, or alongside limited,
direct experience.

To address these gaps, this study develops and tests an integrated model
linking core Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) beliefs, perceived ease
of use and perceived usefulness (Davis, 1989; Venkatesh et al., 2003),
to perceived personalisation and anticipated technology-enabled
immersion, and subsequently to loyalty intentions. The model
incorporates trust as a facilitator of data-enabled tailoring (Gefen et
al., 2003) and privacy concerns as a potential inhibitor via perceived
risk and control considerations (Malhotra et al., 2004; Awad & Krishnan,
2006). Immersion is conceptualised as anticipated,
technology-facilitated absorption and presence enabled by interactive
and vivid media (Sobitan & Vlachos, 2020; Chen et al., 2025), and
loyalty intentions are operationalised as revisit and recommendation
intentions common in festival research (Van Winkle & Falk, 2015; Sisson
& Alcorn, 2022). Empirically, the model is tested with survey data from
festival attendees (N = 338) using covariance-based SEM.

This study contributes by (1) positioning smart festivals as distinctive
hyper-sensory environments where acceptance mechanisms and experiential
mechanisms interact; (2) extending TAM in a festival context by
demonstrating how perceived personalisation functions as a value
mechanism that enables immersion and strengthens loyalty intentions; and
(3) informing the emerging governance debate by examining the role of
trust and privacy concerns in shaping personalisation evaluations,
including the possibility of a privacy--personalisation tension in
data-intensive festival infrastructures (Ciftci et al., 2024; Karwatzki
et al., 2017). The paper concludes with experience design and management
implications for delivering ethical personalisation and meaningful
immersion under peak festival operating conditions.

## Literature Review

### 2.1 Smart festivals and adoption readiness {#smart-festivals-and-adoption-readiness .unnumbered}

Smart festivals constitute transient, high-density events that integrate
digital infrastructures and interactive elements throughout the attendee
experience pathway. In festival environments, the pertinence of these
technologies is heightened by peak demand, queuing, overcrowding, and
temporal pressures, rendering attendees more reliant on real-time
information and services that alleviate friction. Empirical insights
from event tourism underscore how technology adoption can mould the
formation of memorable experiences and subsequent behavioural outcomes
in major cultural events (Shafeeque & Azees, 2024).

Given that a considerable proportion of festival attendees possess
limited prior exposure to advanced festival technologies, this study
centres on adoption readiness by modelling prospective evaluations**,**
anticipated perceptions of usefulness, ease of use, personalisation, and
immersion**,** before direct interaction with these systems. Festivals
also represent value co-creation arenas via interactions among
organisers, attendees, and stakeholders; yet, under peak-load
conditions, suboptimal usability or unclear data practices may convert
smart infrastructures from experiential facilitators into
friction-inducing barriers (Azara et al., 2023; Wallace & Michopoulou,
2023; Michopoulou et al., 2023). Therefore, understanding the nuances of
technology integration in these dynamic environments is crucial for
optimizing visitor engagement and satisfaction (Chen et al., 2025). This
growing emphasis on technology calls for a deeper understanding of how
smart festival technologies, including digital platforms and
data-enabled services such as festival apps, access and payment systems,
real-time information tools, and interactive/immersive touchpoints, are
integrated to support personalised experiences, streamline operations,
and manage crowds more effectively (Neuhofer et al., 2020; Norfolk &
O'Regan, 2020; Lopes et al., 2025). This also includes immersive and
hybrid applications, AR/VR features and interactive installations, that
can enhance engagement before and during attendance, elevating the
overall festival experience (Sjukriana et al., 2024). Such developments
not only shape attendees' leisure experiences but also provide
organisers with opportunities to improve operational efficiency and
introduce new engagement formats (Bossey, 2018; Cimbaljević et al.,
2019).

### 2.2 Core technology acceptance beliefs: perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness {#core-technology-acceptance-beliefs-perceived-ease-of-use-and-perceived-usefulness .unnumbered}

In the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), perceived ease of use denotes
the anticipated degree of effortlessness in employing a technological
system, whereas perceived usefulness signifies the expected enhancement
of performance and attainment of beneficial outcomes. These core beliefs
gain particular salience in festival environments, where they are
intensified by context-specific challenges such as overcrowding,
queuing, temporal pressures, and inconsistent connectivity. Prior
empirical research on technology adoption in events and festivals
demonstrates that TAM constructs serve as primary predictors of
attitudes and intentions toward technology, especially when such
innovations provide evident functional advantages during on-site
participation (Talantis et al., 2020). Supporting evidence from
AI-enabled smart service contexts in hospitality further highlights the
critical role of usability and performance expectancy in forming
consumer receptivity and behavioral intentions, thereby reinforcing the
applicability of TAM principles to technology-mediated experiences.
Specifically, perceived ease of use influences technology adoption by
reducing the cognitive load associated with new systems, while perceived
usefulness drives acceptance by aligning the technology with users\'
goals and desired outcomes (Du et al., 2024;). This dual emphasis on
cognitive accessibility and practical utility is critical in dynamic
festival settings where rapid adoption and seamless integration are
paramount for enhancing attendee satisfaction and operational efficacy
(Bär et al., 2025).

H1: Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU) positively influences Perceived
Usefulness (PU).

### 2.4 Privacy concerns and the personalisation--privacy trade-off {#privacy-concerns-and-the-personalisationprivacy-trade-off .unnumbered}

Privacy concerns encompass individuals\' worries regarding the
gathering, utilization, and dissemination of personal data, coupled with
the sense of diminished control over such information. Within
data-intensive services, the advantages of personalisation are
frequently offset by privacy-related drawbacks, prompting a privacy
calculus whereby users balance prospective benefits against apprehended
privacy hazards (Awad & Krishnan, 2006; Karwatzki et al., 2017). This
dichotomy holds special relevance in smart festival settings, wherein
mechanisms like access management, cashless payments, and
geolocation-based navigation heighten data exposure, rendering
disclosure seemingly urgent and inescapable. Empirical findings from
event studies underscore privacy as a salient impediment in data-heavy
festival innovations; for instance, investigations into biometric
applications at music festivals pinpoint privacy apprehensions as a
primary deterrent alongside anticipated gains (Norfolk & O'Regan, 2020),
whereas analyses of facial recognition in corporate events reveal that
privacy evaluations interact with personalisation assessments to shape
technology adoption decisions (Ciftci et al., 2024). Complementary
research in digital tourism services on the personalisation--privacy
paradox further illustrates how privacy safeguards and risk perceptions
modulate consumer reactions to tailored offerings. Collectively, these
considerations imply that elevated privacy concerns diminish perceived
personalisation in smart festival technologies, recasting customisation
as potentially invasive or hazardous. This creates a tension between the
perceived value of personalized services and the heightened anxiety
regarding the misuse or unauthorized access of personal data, which can
undermine trust in the underlying technology (Priyanka & Siraj, 2021).
This suggests that organizations developing smart services must
proactively address privacy concerns to cultivate user trust, as
perceptions of a provider\'s benevolence are critical for fostering
adoption (Viot et al., 2024). Taken together, these arguments suggest
that higher privacy concerns should reduce perceived personalisation in
smart festival technologies by reframing tailoring as intrusive or
risky.

H2c: Privacy and data concerns (PRIVC) negatively influence Perceived
Personalisation (PERS).

### 2.5 Perceived personalisation as a value mechanism in smart festivals {#perceived-personalisation-as-a-value-mechanism-in-smart-festivals .unnumbered}

Perceived personalisation is conceptualised as the degree to which
festival attendees perceive that the festival\'s technological systems
customise information, recommendations, and operational assistance in
alignment with their individual preferences and requirements. Within
digital services, perceived personalisation enhances perceptions of
relevance and value, thereby bolstering positive user responses (Bleier
& Eisenbeiß, 2015). In tourism and technology-facilitated experiential
contexts, personalisation has been associated with improved evaluations
of experiences and heightened behavioural intentions, owing to augmented
perceptions of suitability, convenience, and overall value (Koo et al.,
2025; Jeong & Shin, 2019; Pai et al., 2020). In the context of smart
festivals, perceived usefulness is anticipated to bolster perceptions of
personalisation, as efficacious systems are more readily interpreted as
providing pertinent, context-sensitive assistance; likewise, trust is
expected to validate personalisation as a benevolent feature rather than
an intrusive one.

H2a: Perceived Usefulness (PU) positively influences Perceived
Personalisation (PERS).

Trust should further facilitate personalisation perceptions in
data-intensive festival environments. Personalised services typically
rely on data collection and processing; therefore, festivalgoers who
trust the technology ecosystem and organiser's stewardship are more
likely to accept and legitimise tailoring as benevolent support, while
mistrust can lead attendees to discount personalised features as
intrusive, manipulative, or unreliable (Gefen et al., 2003; Awad &
Krishnan, 2006). In turn, stronger trust should increase perceived
personalisation.\
H2b: Trust in smart festival technologies (TRUST/CONF) positively
influences Perceived Personalisation (PERS).

### 2.6 Anticipated immersive experience {#anticipated-immersive-experience .unnumbered}

In this study, immersive experience is defined as the anticipated
technology-facilitated absorption and presence, entailing profound
engagement distinguished by elevated attention and sensory immersion,
which interactive and vivid media, such as augmented or virtual reality
(AR/VR), responsive installations, and immersive sensory stimuli, can
engender (Sobitan & Vlachos, 2020; Chen et al., 2025). Scholarship on
events demonstrates that immersive festival experiences bolster identity
construction, memory consolidation, and revisit inclinations within
festival and heritage domains (Iversen et al., 2023), while AR-augmented
interaction points heighten experiential quality and intentions to
return relative to non-AR counterparts (Ngan & Lei, 2024).
Personalisation promotes immersion by alleviating experiential
disruptions and amplifying contextual relevance, thereby enabling
attendees to sustain undivided presence in the immediate moment. This
enhanced presence, facilitated by tailored technological interventions,
optimizes the subjective perception of the festival environment,
transforming it into a more deeply engaging and memorable encounter
(Dieck et al., 2017; Sustacha et al., 2023). In the context of smart
festivals, personalised features are anticipated to alleviate cognitive
load and decision fatigue amid time pressures, thereby enabling
attendees to direct greater attention towards the festival\'s sensory
and social dimensions. By enhancing perceived relevance and mitigating
experiential friction, personalisation is thus expected to reinforce the
anticipated absorption and presence that define immersive experiences.

H3: Perceived Personalisation (PERS) positively influences Immersive
Experience (INM).

### 2.8 Attitude and intention to use smart festival technologies {#attitude-and-intention-to-use-smart-festival-technologies .unnumbered}

Attitude towards utilising smart festival technologies embodies a
holistic evaluative predisposition concerning the integration of the
festival\'s technological framework (Davis, 1989). In technology
acceptance literature, attitude delineates the degree to which users
deem a system\'s employment a \"good idea,\" acting as a principal
immediate antecedent of behavioural intention, particularly for elective
and emergent technologies among users. Within smart festival
environments, this attitudinal perspective is moulded by
context-specific exigencies, such as overcrowding, temporal urgency, and
demands for expeditious on-site choices, that magnify the experiential
ramifications of technology engagement.

Fundamentally, festivals represent hedonic and experience-oriented
contexts. Hence, attitudes towards smart festival technologies are
expected to be shaped not only by utilitarian factors but also by
anticipated experiential benefits. Perceived personalisation enhances
attitudes by delivering customised schedules, recommendations, and
context-sensitive assistance, which heighten perceived relevance and
mitigate cognitive load, thereby positioning the technology as
efficacious rather than commonplace. Similarly, anticipated immersion
bolsters attitudes, as technologies promoting profound absorption and
presence tend to receive positive evaluations in live-event settings,
where emotional involvement and memorability constitute core
determinants of success. This proposition is substantiated by prior
event studies on mobile applications, which reveal that technology
appraisals are strongly linked to favourable attitudes and subsequent
behavioural intentions in event contexts. Moreover, a positive attitude
is poised to elevate the intention to adopt smart festival technologies,
reflecting a deliberate commitment to engage with the technological
ecosystem in future festival encounters (Talantis et al., 2020). This
aligns with previous research indicating that positive attitudes towards
technology are strong predictors of behavioral intentions, particularly
in contexts where technology mediates engaging and satisfying user
experiences (Ahmad et al., 2022).

H4: Perceived Personalisation (PERS) positively influences Attitude
(ACT).

H5: Immersive Experience (INM) positively influences Attitude (ACT).

H6: Attitude (ACT) positively influences Intention to use smart festival
technologies (INT).

### 2.9 Loyalty intention in smart festivals {#loyalty-intention-in-smart-festivals .unnumbered}

Behavioural loyalty intentions (LOY) towards festivals are typically
defined as intentions signifying sustained involvement with the festival
brand, most often measured via revisit intentions and positive
word-of-mouth or recommendations. In festival contexts, loyalty is
predominantly experience-driven, given that the core offering
constitutes a transient, emotionally intense encounter whose positive
appraisals manifest as enduring memories shaping future attendance and
promotional behaviours. This aligns with festival scholarship, which
links profound, memorable experiences, bolstered by immersion,
identity-related dynamics, and narrative immersion, to elevated revisit
intentions and recommendation tendencies (Van Winkle & Falk, 2015;
Iversen et al., 2023). Additionally, technology-facilitated interactions
can augment experiential quality and promote revisit intentions by
fostering deeper engagement while alleviating on-site impediments (Ngan
& Lei, 2024). Therefore, the development of loyalty within the smart
festival paradigm is not solely a function of satisfaction, but rather a
multifaceted outcome influenced by the holistic, technology-enhanced
experience (Wang et al., 2024). Specifically, consumers\' behavioral
intentions are closely linked to their overall satisfaction and
perceived quality of the event (Lamari & Bouslama, 2025), with higher
satisfaction directly translating into increased behavioral intentions
within a festival context. This suggests that positive interactions with
smart festival technologies, leading to enhanced experiences and
satisfaction, are crucial for fostering sustained loyalty behaviors
(Muanas et al., 2025).

In technology-mediated festival settings, loyalty may be bolstered via
acceptance-driven and experience-driven mechanisms. Firstly, the
intention to utilise the festival\'s technology ecosystem signifies
preparedness to interact with digital interfaces on-site; such
intentions enable attendees to gain from enhanced access, superior
navigation and scheduling, along with prompt informational aid, thereby
optimising the overall attendee journey and elevating propensities for
revisitation and endorsements (Davis, 1989; Talantis et al., 2020).
Secondly, perceived personalisation bolsters loyalty by augmenting
perceived relevance and congruence between festival provisions and
participants\' preferences, thus cultivating a deeper psychological bond
with the festival encounter (Bleier & Eisenbeiß, 2015; Jeong & Shin,
2019). Thirdly, immersive experience directly fosters loyalty by
amplifying emotional involvement, sense of presence, and memorability,
core processes underpinning post-event promotion and recurrent
participation in festival milieus (Sobitan & Vlachos, 2020; Iversen et
al., 2023; Ngan & Lei, 2024).

H7: Intention to use smart festival technologies (INT) positively
influences Behavioural Loyalty Intention (LOY).

H8: Perceived Personalisation (PERS) positively influences Behavioural
Loyalty Intention (LOY).

H9: Immersive Experience (INM) positively influences Behavioural Loyalty
Intention (LOY).

# Methodology

## 3.1 Study design  {#study-design .unnumbered}

This study adopted a quantitative, cross-sectional survey design to
examine festival attendees' adoption readiness for smart festivals
technologies and their anticipated effects on personalisation, immersion
and loyalty outcomes. Because a substantial share of respondents had
limited direct exposure to immersive or AI-enabled festival
technologies, the measures capture anticipated evaluations (i.e.,
prospective usefulness, personalisation and immersion) rather than
purely post-consumption assessments. This positioning is consistent with
technology adoption research examining pre-use beliefs and intention
formation in experiential contexts (Davis, 1989; Venkatesh et al.,
2003).

The study is positioned in the broader context of live festivals where
attendees face peak demand, queues, crowding, time pressure and
wayfinding needs. To reduce ambiguity in prospective evaluations,
respondents were instructed to think about live festivals they attend
and the types of smart-festival technologies increasingly used in these
settings (festival apps, digital ticketing, access control, QR, RFID
wearables, cashless payments, real-time updates, and immersive
activations).

## 3.2 Questionnaire and measures {#questionnaire-and-measures .unnumbered}

The questionnaire was developed to capture respondents' previous
experience with live festivals and technologies, their general
familiarity with new technologies, and their evaluations of smart
technologies used in festival settings. Multi-item constructs were
measured using seven-point Likert-type scales ranging from 1 ("strongly
disagree") to 7 ("strongly agree"). The instrument began with an
informed consent statement and assurances of anonymity and
confidentiality, followed by sections on festival attendance, experience
with festival technologies, main construct scales and sociodemographic
questions.

The evaluation of Perceived usefulness (PU) and perceived ease of use
(PEOU) were each measured with four reflective items adapted from prior
technology acceptance research in digital event contexts (Bär et al.,
2025), consistent with the original TAM conceptualisation (Davis, 1989).

Perceived personalisation (PERS) was measured with four items capturing
the extent to which festival technologies are perceived to tailor
information and recommendations and support user-controlled
customisation, adapted from smart tourism and personalisation research
(Koo et al., 2025; Jeong & Shin, 2020).

Anticipated immersive experience (INM) was measured with three items
capturing perceived absorption and deep engagement enabled by immersive
and interactive festival touchpoints, adapted from immersive experience
research in event contexts (Chen et al., 2025).

Trust in smart festival technologies (CONF) was measured with three
items adapted from the integrated trust--TAM framework (Gefen et al.,
2003), reflecting beliefs about reliability and acting in the user's
interest.

Privacy and data concerns (PRIVC) were measured with three items adapted
from the IUIPC scale (Malhotra et al., 2004), capturing concerns about
data collection and use.

Attitude toward using smart festival technologies (ACT) was measured
with three items reflecting overall evaluative orientation toward
adoption, adapted from recent smart-service acceptance research in
hospitality contexts (Du et al., 2025).\
Intention to use smart festival technologies (INT) was measured with
three items adapted from event-related technology acceptance research,
including prior work applying extended TAM in a festival context (Lee et
al., 2012).\
Behavioral loyalty intention (LOY) was measured with three items
capturing revisit and recommendation intentions, adapted from
festival/tourism loyalty research (Lee et al., 2012; Jeong & Shin,
2020).

Prior to the main data collection, the questionnaire was piloted with 45
individuals who had experience attending live festivals. Based on the
pretest results, two academic scholars reviewed and refined the
questions. All constructs were modelled as reflective latent variables.
Reliability was assessed using Cronbach's alpha (Hair et al. 2010).
During measurement evaluation, two items: "I would like to use smart
technologies at future festivals" in Attitude Toward Smart Technologies
in Festivals and "I predict that I would use smart technologies if they
were available at a festivals" in Intention to use Smart Technologies at
Festivals showed inadequate psychometric performance. Consistent with
scale purification guidelines, these items were removed from the
measurement model and excluded from further analyses to improve
reliability and convergent validity. The remaining indicators for each
construct exhibited satisfactory reliability and validity.

## 3.3 Sampling, data collection, and controls {#sampling-data-collection-and-controls .unnumbered}

Fieldwork for this research took place between November 2025 and
February 2026 and was facilitated by an online questionnaire
disseminated through social media and online communities relevant to
live-festival audiences. Thus, the sample was drawn through
non-probability, purposive sampling. Respondents were 18 years of age or
older.

To ensure relevance to the festival context, the survey included a
screening item asking whether respondents had attended any live festival
within the past two years. Participants who did not meet this criterion
were directed to the end of the questionnaire.

An a priori power analysis using G\*Power indicated a minimum required
sample size of N = 205. Given that SEM typically requires larger samples
than single-equation regression, and following conservative guidance on
sample size considerations for covariance-based SEM (Reinartz et al.,
2009), we adopted a target of at least N = 305. The final sample (N =
338) therefore exceeds both the minimum power requirement and this
conservative benchmark, supporting adequate statistical power and
reliable parameter estimation for the CB-SEM analyses.

Because the survey survey used a forced-response format, the dataset
contained no missing values. Accordingly, CFA and SEM were estimated
using complete cases (listwise deletion), which in practice corresponds
to analysing the full available sample for the measurement and
structural models. Model estimation converged without issues and fit
indices were within acceptable ranges.

As the study relies on a self-reported, cross-sectional survey, we took
steps to reduce potential response biases. The questionnaire used clear,
non-technical wording and emphasised that there were no right or wrong
answers, encouraging honest responses. Post-hoc common method bias
diagnostics suggested CMV is unlikely to be a major concern. Harman's
single-factor test indicated that the first factor accounted for 44.7%
of the variance (\< 50%). Further confirming the absence of CMV, a
one-factor CFA showed poor fit (CFI = 0.644, RMSEA = 0.150, SRMR =

## Discussion 

This research advances the field of smart festival studies by
demonstrating how festival operational conditions enhance technology
acceptance processes and mould expectations of personalisation and
technology-mediated immersion. These results align with the extant
literature, which emphasises the critical role of stakeholder needs
alongside value co-creation and co-destruction in driving the success of
contemporary festivals. (Azara et al., 2023; Wallace & Michopoulou,
2023; Michopoulou et al., 2023). Furthermore, this research contributes
by providing a robust theoretical framework for understanding the
intricate interplay between festival attributes, attendee perceptions,
and loyalty intentions within technology-enriched festival environments,
addressing prior limitations regarding generalizability (Tanford & Jung,
2017). Specifically, the mediating role of place attachment further
elucidates how satisfactory experiences foster emotional connections and
behavioral intentions toward festival host communities (Lee et al.,
2012). This suggests that place attachment, alongside festival
satisfaction and co-creation experiences, plays a crucial role in
influencing festival evaluation and subsequent re-patronizing intention
(Zhang et al., 2019). This comprehensive framework underscores the
importance of integrating motivational dimensions to fully comprehend
the cultural tourism experience, advocating for culturally engaging
programs and innovative technology to enhance satisfaction and cultivate
loyalty in cultural events (Carvache‐Franco et al., 2025).

Overall, the findings endorse an integrated pathway combining technology
acceptance and experiential dimensions in smart festivals. Perceived
ease of use substantially bolstered perceived usefulness, while
usefulness and trust constituted primary predictors of perceived
personalisation. Personalisation, in turn, amplified anticipated
immersion and shaped loyalty intentions both directly and indirectly
through attitude and intention to use. These dynamics align with
contemporary event tourism research indicating that technology adoption
fosters memorable experiences and subsequent behavioural outcomes (Hanaa
& P, 2024), as well as festival studies associating experiential quality
with loyalty and word-of-mouth (Sisson & Alcorn, 2021). Accordingly,
this study advances extant theory by exploring novel constructs and
empirically validating their interrelationships within the smart
festival context (Swart et al., 2019). Moreover, the framework
synthesises principles from the Technology Acceptance Model and the
Experience Economy Framework, thereby providing a comprehensive lens for
understanding how technology-enabled tourism offerings enrich attendee
experiences and cultivate loyalty in smart tourism destinations (Chhabra
et al., 2025).

### 5.1 Theoretical contribution {#theoretical-contribution .unnumbered}

This study advances festival and event scholarship in three principal
ways. First, it conceptualises smart festivals as unique organisational
contexts wherein operational conditions intensify technology acceptance
processes. In these settings, minor usability impediments can rapidly
result in overlooked performances and eroded perceived value, thereby
heightening the applied significance of ease-of-use and usefulness
perceptions. This underscores the imperative for intuitive digital
interfaces and resilient technological infrastructure to enable fluid
incorporation into the festival experience (Xiong & Zhang, 2024).

Secondly, the results elucidate the transformation of 'smartness' into
experiential value within festival contexts: perceived personalisation
serves as a key value mechanism that translates technology-related
beliefs into immersive engagement and loyalty intentions (Çiğdemli &
Avci, 2022; Hlee & Lee, 2022). This highlights the importance of value
co-creation through stakeholder interactions and resource integration
(Azara et al., 2023; Wallace & Michopoulou, 2023; Michopoulou et al.,
2023).

Third, the results sharpen the conceptual relationship between
technology-enabled immersion and behavioural outcomes in festivals. The
proposed model demonstrates that immersive experiences promote
behavioural loyalty both directly and indirectly, mediated by more
positive attitudes and heightened intentions to adopt smart festival
technologies. This advances existing festival literature, which connects
immersion to identity development, memory formation, and revisit
intentions, by delineating the pivotal role of technology-mediated
interactions in facilitating such immersive outcomes (Sobitan & Vlachos,
2020; Iversen et al., 2023). This alignment reinforces the concept that
advanced digital tools, particularly those offering personalization and
accessibility, are instrumental in shaping both hedonic and utilitarian
consumption motivations within tourism experiences (Yahya et al., 2026).

### 5.3 Practical implications {#practical-implications .unnumbered}

The findings offer clear implications for smart festival design and
management. A central priority is to implement personalisation as
situational relevance rather than preference matching alone. Tailoring
that reduces friction under peak conditions---such as dynamic
itineraries, moment-based recommendations, congestion alerts, and quiet
or alternative routes---can lower cognitive load and decision fatigue
during attendance. In practice, this implies designing personalised
recommendations for services such as optimal navigation routes or
real-time crowd-sourced insights to enhance the attendee journey and
minimise negative experiential aspects (Ramli et al., 2023). Such a
proactive approach leverages data analytics to deliver relevant
information at opportune moments, thereby mitigating information
overload and improving the overall visitor experience (Lei et al.,
2022).

In parallel, immersive design should be approached as an experience
strategy that is embedded within the festival's theme, identity and
storytelling. Interactive installations and AR/VR touchpoints can deepen
presence and emotional engagement, but they are most valuable when
technological novelty is translated into meaningful experience and
memory formation. Leveraging virtual reality to provide immersive
experiences may also attract new and younger audiences, extending the
festival's reach and strengthening engagement (Gallarza et al., 2023).
Moreover, personalised and interactive AI features, such as tailored
activity suggestions or gamification elements, can enhance emotional and
experiential value, contributing to a more enjoyable and meaningful user
experience (Nookhao & Homsud, 2025). This strategic integration of
advanced digital technologies, including virtual reality, can support
sophisticated experiential marketing strategies that resonate with
diverse customer segments (Fernandes & Krolikowska, 2022; Lee et al.,
2022). Beyond increasing engagement and cultural exploration, it can
also enable dynamic narratives where attendees create and share
personalised experiences, extending the festival's value proposition
beyond its physical boundaries. Over time, such design choices can
foster a co-creative environment in which the festival narrative is not
merely consumed but actively shaped by participants, leading to stronger
emotional resonance and self-renewal (Wang et al., 2025).

Finally, trust and privacy should be treated as integral components of
the festival experience rather than background compliance. Reliability
and data governance can be strengthened through clear explanations of
data use, fine-grained consent mechanisms, and opt-out options that do
not restrict access to essential services. Operationally, robust support
protocols are needed to manage disruptions during periods of peak demand
so that service failures do not trigger co-destruction dynamics. This
requires transparent communication regarding data utilisation and
empowering users with meaningful control over personal information,
which supports the trust needed for sustained engagement within digital
tourism ecosystems (Orea-Giner et al., 2022). More broadly, leveraging
digital advancements to build robust infrastructure, provide
personalised services, and implement sophisticated digital marketing and
analytics can enhance attractiveness and visitor satisfaction (Vujko et
al., 2025). Taken together, a smart festival approach that combines
situationally relevant personalisation, narrative-integrated immersion,
and trust-by-design can contribute to subjective well-being and
strengthen loyalty within the festival tourism ecosystem (Zhang & Deng,
2022).

## 5.4 Limitations and future research  {#limitations-and-future-research .unnumbered}

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the sample is
non-probabilistic and includes a large student share; thus,
generalisation should be cautious. Second, all measures are
self-reported and collected in a single survey; although procedural
remedies and statistical diagnostics suggest common method variance is
unlikely to be the primary driver, some shared-method influence cannot
be entirely ruled out. Third, the design is cross-sectional and focuses
on anticipated evaluations, which precludes causal claims and may differ
from post-experience evaluations after actual technology use.

Future research avenues include extending these findings through diverse
methodological approaches. Model validation should employ on-site or
longitudinal studies encompassing pre-event anticipations, in-event
experiential dynamics, and post-event loyalty metrics, incorporating
behavioral measures. Comparative analyses across festival variants and
boundary condition tests, such as crowd density levels, network
stability, and technological advancement, are recommended. Moderators
elucidating the privacy-personalization paradox warrant investigation.
Moreover, the framework may be augmented to address ethical and
inclusivity facets, including perceived equity, accessibility
provisions, and heterogeneous effects of data-intensive personalization
among attendee cohorts.
